# Vendor Master File and Payments Policy

## 1. Scope
This policy covers how auditors review a client's vendor (supplier) master file and the payments made
to vendors. It is used together with the vendor segmentation produced by the AuditLens platform.

## 2. Vendor onboarding controls we expect
A well-controlled client requires, for every new vendor: a completed onboarding form, verification of
the vendor's tax identification number, independent confirmation of bank details by a call-back to a
known telephone number, and approval by someone who cannot post payments. Changes to vendor bank details
must be approved by two people.

## 3. Segregation of duties
The same person must not be able to create a vendor and approve payments to that vendor. Where a client
cannot segregate these duties because of its size, the auditor documents the compensating control, such
as a monthly owner review of all new vendors and payments.

## 4. Master file analytics
At least once per engagement the team runs these tests on the vendor master file: duplicate vendors
(same tax ID, bank account or address), vendors sharing a bank account or address with an employee,
vendors with no activity for more than 18 months, and vendors created and paid within 7 days.

## 5. Behavioural vendor segments
AuditLens groups vendors by behaviour (payment volume, average amount, amount volatility, share of
night-time activity, and number of distinct payers). Segments are a planning tool: a vendor in a
high-risk segment is not evidence of wrongdoing, but vendors in segments with high night-time activity
or very erratic amounts are prioritised for detailed testing.

## 6. Dormant vendors
Vendors with no payments for more than 18 months should be deactivated. A payment to a dormant vendor
is a red flag and must be traced to an approved invoice.

## 7. Escalation
Suspected fictitious vendors are escalated to the engagement manager the same business day.
