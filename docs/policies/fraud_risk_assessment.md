# Fraud Risk Assessment Guidance

## 1. Why we assess fraud risk
Auditors plan and perform the engagement with professional scepticism, recognising that a material
misstatement due to fraud could exist even where past experience suggests management is honest.
Fraud risk is assessed at the planning stage and updated whenever new information arises.

## 2. The fraud triangle
The Firm uses the fraud triangle to structure discussions. Three conditions are usually present when
fraud occurs: an incentive or pressure to commit fraud, an opportunity to carry it out, and an attitude
or rationalisation that allows the person to justify it. Weak internal controls are the most common
source of opportunity.

## 3. Engagement team discussion
Every engagement team holds a fraud brainstorming discussion before fieldwork begins. The engagement
partner leads it and all team members attend. The discussion covers how and where the client's
financial statements could be susceptible to fraud, including management override of controls.

## 4. Presumed risks
Two fraud risks are presumed on every audit engagement unless the team documents why they do not apply:
revenue recognition, and management override of controls. The presumption about management override
can never be rebutted.

## 5. Journal entry testing
To address management override, the team tests journal entries. Entries with these characteristics
are selected for testing: entries posted outside business hours or on weekends; entries posted by
senior management or by users who do not normally post entries; round-number entries; entries made
to unusual or rarely used accounts; and entries posted after the period-end close. At least 25 journal
entries are tested on every engagement, and all entries above performance materiality that meet a
risk characteristic are tested.

## 6. Red flags in payment data
Common red flags include: payments that empty an account in a single transaction; transfers followed
immediately by cash withdrawals; payments just below an approval limit; duplicate invoice numbers;
vendors sharing a bank account or address with an employee; and unusual activity at night.

## 7. Responding to identified fraud
If the team identifies a suspected fraud, it must not alert the suspected individual. The engagement
partner informs the Firm's General Counsel within 1 business day and communicates the matter to those
charged with governance at the client, unless they are suspected of involvement.
