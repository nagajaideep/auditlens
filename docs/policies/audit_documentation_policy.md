# Audit Documentation and Evidence Policy

## 1. Purpose
Audit documentation provides the evidence for the auditor's conclusions and shows that the work was
planned and performed in accordance with professional standards.

## 2. What must be documented
Documentation must allow an experienced auditor with no previous connection to the engagement to
understand the nature, timing and extent of procedures performed, the results and evidence obtained,
and the significant judgements made. Each workpaper records who prepared it and the date, and who
reviewed it and the date.

## 3. Assembly deadline
The final engagement file must be assembled within 45 days after the date of the audit report. After
that date no documentation may be deleted. Any later additions must state the date added, who added it
and the reason.

## 4. Retention period
Engagement documentation is retained for seven years from the date of the audit report.

## 5. Sufficiency and appropriateness of evidence
Evidence is more reliable when obtained from independent external sources, when obtained directly by
the auditor, and when it exists in documentary form. Oral representations from management alone are
not sufficient evidence for material matters.

## 6. Evidence from data analytics
When analytics or models are used, the workpaper must record the data source, the completeness and
accuracy checks performed on the data, the model version used, the parameters or threshold applied, and
how each exception was followed up.

## 7. Use of generative AI
Output from generative AI tools may be included in workpapers only after review by a team member, who
signs off that each statement is supported by the cited source. Client confidential data must not be
entered into a generative AI tool that is not approved by the Firm.
