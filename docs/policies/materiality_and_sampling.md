# Materiality and Sampling Policy

## 1. Overall materiality
Overall materiality is set using a benchmark appropriate to the entity. Default benchmarks are 5% of
profit before tax for profit-oriented entities, 1% of total revenue when profit is volatile or close to
zero, and 2% of total assets for asset-based entities such as investment funds. The engagement partner
must approve the benchmark and document the rationale.

## 2. Performance materiality
Performance materiality is set between 50% and 75% of overall materiality. The lower end (50%) is used
for first-year audits, entities with a history of misstatements, or where control deficiencies have been
identified. Otherwise 75% may be used.

## 3. Clearly trivial threshold
Misstatements below 5% of overall materiality are treated as clearly trivial and are not accumulated.
All other identified misstatements are recorded on the summary of unadjusted differences.

## 4. Sampling approach
Statistical sampling is used where a population contains more than 250 items. For smaller populations
the team may use non-statistical judgemental selection. Monetary unit sampling is the default method
for testing balances for overstatement.

## 5. Sample sizes
For tests of controls, the minimum sample sizes are: 25 items for a control operating daily or more
often, 5 items for a weekly control, 2 items for a monthly control and 2 items for a quarterly control.
An annual control is tested once. For substantive tests the sample size depends on the confidence
level required: 95% confidence for a significant risk and 90% confidence otherwise.

## 6. Use of analytics instead of sampling
Where the full population is available electronically, the team may test 100% of transactions with data
analytics and machine learning instead of sampling. Items flagged by analytics must still be followed
up individually; analytics does not replace obtaining evidence for exceptions.

## 7. Evaluating results
If a sample shows a deviation rate above the tolerable rate, the control cannot be relied upon and the
team must extend substantive procedures.
